# KB Ventures: design brief

1. **Who it is for:** local business owners (a barber, a plumber, a cafe owner) deciding whether to trust us with their website, and parents of Year 10 and VCE students looking at study guides. Busy people who read on their phone and want to know quickly who we are and what we do.
2. **The one idea: a printer's proof sheet.** The page reads like a proof from a small local print shop: ink on warm paper, set in big confident type, with the demo work laid out as pinned proofs (crop marks, plain captions) and a few marks in the editor's red pencil. Nothing glows, floats or shines.
3. **Palette (2 colours plus a neutral):** Press ink `#1C1A17` (text and the dark footer), proof red `#B83418` (links, marks, one word in the hero; 5:1 on paper, so it also works for small text; a lighter `#E9583A` only on ink), and paper `#F1ECE2` as the neutral, with a muted ink `#5E5850` for captions.
4. **Type pairing:** Instrument Serif for headlines (a tall, tightly set display serif, used very large, with its italic for emphasis) and Schibsted Grotesk for text (a sturdy newspaper grotesk, 17–19px body, small caps-style labels at 13px with tracking). Both self-hosted under the SIL Open Font License.
5. **Rules we follow:** an asymmetric 12-column grid with a narrow label column on the left; whitespace instead of boxes; hairline rules instead of cards; every section laid out differently; motion only for one load sequence, small reveals and hover states, all switched off for reduced motion.

## Six techniques noted from studio sites (locomotive.ca, basement.studio, pentagram.com)
1. **Narrow label column, wide content column.** Locomotive sets a small sans label ("Articles", "Culture") on the left of a hairline and lets the content start far to the right, so the grid is visibly asymmetric.
2. **Oversized type mixed with very small type.** Headlines and project names run huge (Locomotive's work list, basement's statement lines) right beside 13–14px captions, with almost nothing in between.
3. **Cropped images with plain captions under them.** Pentagram crops project images hard to fill the frame and puts the name and a one-line description underneath in small type, aligned to the image's left edge, never on top of it.
4. **Hover states that reveal rather than decorate.** Underlines draw in, arrows shift a few pixels, images scale very slowly inside a fixed frame; nothing bounces or glows.
5. **Section changes by switching the whole ground.** Pentagram moves from white to a full charcoal panel and back; the colour change, not a divider, marks the new section.
6. **The footer as a set piece.** A huge wordmark cropped by the bottom edge of the page (Pentagram, basement), the address and email set as display type (Locomotive), with link lists in small type over hairlines.
